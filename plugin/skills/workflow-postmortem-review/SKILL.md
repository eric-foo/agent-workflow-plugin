# Workflow Postmortem Review

Run a structural integrity review of completed work. The goal is to prevent a false or overbroad success claim from carrying weak evidence, stale next-action pointers, stage confusion, unclassified residue, or process damage into the next step.

This skill protects long-term repo/workflow integrity by testing whether completed work leaves truthful evidence, clear boundaries, explicit residue, and safe continuity. Findings are about claims, evidence, boundaries, and process effects, not fault or intent.

Default to review/report mode. Do not patch, push, merge, release, delete residue, or reopen design decisions unless the user explicitly asks for that follow-up work.

## Core Rule

Review the completed unit as a chain of claims and evidence, not as a narrative summary. Every material PASS, complete, ready, clean, pushed, released, validated, or next-ready claim must name the evidence that supports it and the boundary of what it does not prove.

For a completed unit that claims a testable remediation was fixed, the
strongest single evidence pattern is red-green proof: the same named test,
fixture, check, or validation gate fails against the pre-fix baseline for the
reviewed failure mode and passes after the fix. When a new test or fixture is
part of the remediation, the proof should show the test-only or check-only
state failing against the unfixed behavior before the implementation change
makes it pass.

Apply this claim-level contract: advisory integrity analysis may proceed from visible evidence, but strict-shaped claims such as PASS, validated, ready, approved, released, deployed, resolver-visible, plugin-ready, or safe-to-proceed require the relevant bound authority and evidence. Missing authority returns `not proven` or a precise `BLOCKED_*` result. Reading more source cannot create that authority. Plugin, deployment, resolver, and readiness claims are not made without lifecycle evidence. `NOT_CLAIMED` is a non-pass, not a soft approval.

Use these claim classes:

- `advisory`: visible-evidence integrity finding.
- `strict`: pass, complete, clean, validated, approved, ready, or safe-to-proceed claim.
- `lifecycle`: released, deployed, installed, plugin-ready, resolver-visible, or external-publication claim.
- `governance`: workflow-stage, policy-change, source-of-truth, or durable-disposition claim.

Use this evidence provenance ladder when judging proof strength:

```text
authoritative artifact > raw validation output > generated report > summary > conversation claim
```

A weaker source may orient the review, but it does not prove a strict, lifecycle, or governance claim when stronger authority is required.

Raw validation output that shows same-check red-green proof is stronger than a
post-fix-only pass for a testable remediation claim. If red-green proof is
unavailable, inapplicable, or too expensive for the bound lane, classify the
replacement evidence by what it actually proves and state the proof boundary.
Do not treat weaker evidence as equivalent unless the active overlay explicitly
accepts that gate.

## Minimum Evidence Packet

Start with the smallest complete packet needed for the specific claim being reviewed. Do not treat this list as mandatory for every postmortem. Collect an item only when the claim depends on it:

- current branch, head, status, or equivalent workspace state
- recent commit or change boundary
- the artifact, release, state file, or summary that claims completion
- validation outputs named by the claim
- review reports or approval records named by the claim
- next-action or transition record, when one exists
- known deferred work, residue, or external-action state

If a source is huge, extract only decision-relevant lines first. Save or request large raw hits outside live context when the environment supports it.

## Expansion Triggers

Default to compact mode. Expand sources and output only when one of these appears:

- source conflict, missing authority, or hash/version mismatch
- success claim broader than available evidence
- stale owner, release, branch, or next-action pointer
- validation output missing, generated by the wrong path, or too weak for the claim it supports
- a testable remediation claim lacks same-check red-green proof and the
  replacement evidence is not explicitly bounded
- visible evidence suggests a material unclaimed risk outside the claimed
  success state, or a material surface needed for proceed or safe-to-proceed
  language was not inspected
- stage-boundary confusion, such as review editing source, execution reopening design, or cleanup making scope decisions
- unclassified dirty state, residue, skipped work, external publish state, or deferred follow-up
- process-cost concern, such as duplicate review loops, broad source loading, brittle automation, or a recurring stage with repeated no-op findings
- repeatable failure mode that should become a machine-checkable guard or validation fixture

## Review Axes

Check these axes in order:

1. Claim ledger: list the major completion claims, supporting evidence, owner/source of truth, and proof boundary.
2. Evidence strength: classify each claim as supported, adequate, weak, missing, overbroad, not proven, or blocked. Watch for evidence laundering through summaries, deterministic checks being overstated, and same-thread assumptions replacing independent evidence.
3. Unclaimed risk scan: lightly inspect visible evidence and changed surfaces
   for material risks outside the claimed success state, including user impact,
   domain correctness, security/privacy, performance/reliability,
   compatibility/migration, maintainability, and reversibility. Classify as
   `none_visible`, `concern`, or `not_inspected`, with the inspected surface and
   proof boundary. Do not turn this into a full implementation, code, security,
   artifact, or health review unless the user or overlay binds that review lane.
4. Stage boundaries: verify review, execution, validation, cleanup, release, and transition roles did not collapse into each other. Watch for process patches silently bundled into content, validation, or release work.
5. Continuity: verify next action, deferred work, external-action state, and residue are explicit enough for the next actor. Watch for ready language that hides push, merge, release, deployment, cleanup, or approval still pending.
6. Structural health: lightly check auditability, fake-pass risk, stage-boundary health, next-actor clarity, and residue classification every time. Identify changes that make future workflows harder, less auditable, more subjective, or easier to fake-pass.
7. Process cost: check lightly by default. Expand only when stages, source loads, prompts, scripts, or artifacts have visible cost whose observed failure prevention may not justify that cost. Watch for one successful or failed example being overgeneralized into permanent workflow policy.

## Overlay-Owned Evidence

The active local overlay owns source hierarchy, protected paths, required validation commands, local review lanes, evidence labels, and next-action vocabulary. If required overlay authority is missing, return a blocked result instead of inventing evidence.

## Integrity Impact

These are lane-local completed-work integrity labels. They are not artifact
review verdicts, implementation review verdicts, approval, readiness,
validation success, mandatory remediation, patch authority, or executor-ready
handoff.

- `critical`: false pass, hidden unresolved risk, missing authority for a strict/lifecycle/governance claim, or fake-pass path that would mislead the next actor.
- `major`: weak evidence, stale transition state, unclassified residue, stage confusion, or overbroad success language with structural risk.
- `minor`: wording, clarity, or local continuity issue without material structural risk.

## Machine-Checkable Guards

Subjective postmortem judgment complements deterministic validation; it does not replace it. When a finding describes a repeatable or machine-checkable failure mode, prefer recommending a validation fixture, guard, or source contract over adding another review step.

When a completed unit includes a testable fix claim, check whether the evidence
shows same-check red-green proof. Missing red-green proof is not automatically
a failure, but it is weak or bounded evidence unless the claim is inherently
non-testable or the overlay accepted another gate. Treat unbounded post-fix-only
passes as fake-pass risk when they are used to prove the original failure mode
was actually exercised.

## Patch And Simplification Policy

Use findings to recommend keep, narrow, trigger, merge, or remove workflow stages only when the evidence supports that workflow change. Do not remove a stage solely because one postmortem found no issues. Prefer repeated evidence, a named failure mode, and a reversible migration path.

If patches are authorized, separate them from the completed work being reviewed unless the active local overlay explicitly says otherwise. A postmortem patch should have its own boundary and should not make the reviewed work appear as if it originally completed without the finding.

## Durable Disposition

Use durable disposition only in expanded mode, and only for repeated, reusable, policy-affecting, lifecycle-affecting, or testable failure modes. Classify the finding by the smallest complete durable outcome it would need if a later turn authorizes durable follow-up:

- `source_patch`: the finding would require an authorized change to a reusable method, skill candidate, lane contract, overlay contract, or index.
- `validation_fixture`: the finding describes a repeatable failure mode that would be worth testing.
- `governance_record`: the finding would require a governance record to accept, reject, narrow, supersede, or defer a workflow claim.
- `no_durable_change`: the finding is one-off residue or already covered by existing source.

Postmortem may recommend maintenance dispositions for repo maps,
source-loading rules, task-local context pack contracts, context-management
rules, validation fixtures, or governance records when completed-work evidence
shows a repeated, reusable, policy-affecting, lifecycle-affecting, or testable
failure mode. It does not maintain those artifacts directly, does not create a
standing maintenance ceremony, and does not authorize source patches or saved
records by itself.

Do not create a standing postmortem directory or saved postmortem artifact unless a later prompt explicitly binds that destination and repeated accepted records prove the extra structure is needed.

## Output

Return chat-first output by default. Keep the report readable, omit empty
sections, and use stable labels rather than a full YAML envelope unless the
user, overlay, or destination explicitly asks for machine-readable output.

For compact mode, use this order:

- `Integrity result`: `PASS`, `PASS_WITH_FOLLOWUP`, `NEEDS_PATCH`, or `BLOCKED`.
- `Reviewed unit`.
- `Claimed success state`.
- `Claim ledger`: one row per material claim, written as `claim | class |
  evidence | source of truth | proof boundary | status`.
- `Unclaimed risk scan`: `none_visible`, `concern`, or `not_inspected`, with
  inspected surface and proof boundary.
- `Findings`: include only critical, major, or minor integrity-impact groups
  that have content.
- `Structural health`: auditability, fake-pass risk, stage-boundary health,
  next-actor clarity, and residue classification. Use `ok`, `concern`, or
  `unknown`, and include the short basis for each value.
- `Proof boundary`.
- `Proceed status`.
- `Next action`.

Use expanded mode only when an expansion trigger appears. Add only the sections
that carry evidence or routing value: expansion reason, evidence provenance
notes, overbroad success claims, unclaimed risk scan notes, process-cost
findings, durable dispositions, patches recommended, patches applied, residual
risks, and any blocked authority or missing-source details.

If a machine-routable response is required, keep it minimal: use the same field
names above, include only populated fields, and avoid nested YAML templates
unless the destination requires them.

Use `proceed_status` as an evidence-bound continuation statement, not a readiness certification:

- `supported`: the next action is supported within the stated proof boundary.
- `limited_followup`: continuation is reasonable only with named residue or follow-up.
- `not_proven`: the evidence does not support a proceed or safe-to-proceed claim.
- `blocked`: required authority, lifecycle evidence, validation evidence, or source authority is missing.

Use `PASS` only when the reviewed unit's success claims are supported, proof
boundaries are explicit, residue/deferred work is classified, the unclaimed risk
scan has no material `concern`, and `not_inspected` does not cover a material
surface needed for proceed or safe-to-proceed language. Use
`PASS_WITH_FOLLOWUP`, `NEEDS_PATCH`, or `BLOCKED` when that is more precise.
